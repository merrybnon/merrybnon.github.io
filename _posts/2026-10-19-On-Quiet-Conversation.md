The other day, I was lying in bed trying to figure out what to name my blog. A very important decision, as a good name can make or break your blog almost as much as actually good writing. As you may know, I’m a fan of old philosophy, and a reference to something in that realm seemed fitting and pretentious enough for a good blog name. My first idea was actually the secondary title of this blog “The Dao That Can Be Named”. The Dao De Jing famously starts with the line “The Dao that can be named // Is not the Dao”, although this is really a misquote, “naming” comes in the second line, and this drops the “eternal” before the second Dao. Misquote or not, it’s a line I often think about when I’m thinking about modeling reality, or our pursuit of “truth” in general, which is something I think about a lot. I certainly lean to the skeptic side, but this is really an open question in my philosophy, and a full discussion will have to wait for another day. To explain the joke, everything I write is the Dao that can be named, so the blog name is my acknowledgement that as much as I will seek to write something interesting about the secrets of the universe, it will always just be what I can name, it will never be the Dao. 

But “The Dao That Can Be Named” is a much too cumbersome title, and my blog is never going to take off and get me on Hot Ones with that name. So, I needed something snappier. Lately, I’ve been reading/rereading through Seneca’s Moral Letters to Lucilius. When I originally read it in senior year of high school, I read the highlights (as chosen by my teacher), and I walked away with a high opinion of Seneca and a changed worldview. Now reading through a more randomly chosen selection, it can be hit and miss. On the Usefulness of Basic Principles (letter 95) found him ranting about oysters making you lazy (because they eat and create slime, obviously) and having multiple foods in one dish was a clear sign of societal decay. Immediately after, I read On Facing Hardships, and I was like oh he’s back. The long life is like a long journey, of course there will be mud and rain along the way.

Either way, the book seemed like the perfect spot to find my ancient philosophy reference blog title. I found the perfect title in On Quiet Conversation, (dropped the “on”, it’s cleaner), and the letter turned out to be a banger. I’d recommend you read it on your own, it’s quite short, but for a summary, Seneca expounds the benefit of learning through quiet conversation, that even though the seed of reason is small, in the right mind it will take hold and flower into beautiful thought. What is my blog if not scattering my seeds of reason into the wind, hoping they find a friendly audience to mature with? To quote the letter’s last sentences, “Only, as I said, let a favorable mind receive and assimilate them. Then of itself the mind also will produce bounteously in its turn, giving back more than it has received. Farewell”.
